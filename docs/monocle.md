# Wedge Monocle

A camera-only rangefinder for wedge shots (0 to 120 yards). Point the phone at the flagstick, get a steady yardage and the wedge and swing to hit. Everything is computed on the device. No video or images are recorded or uploaded, and no course database or LiDAR is used.

Route: `/monocle` (full screen, lazy-loaded). Entry points: the Rangefinder page and the Practice page.

## How the distance is estimated

A flagstick has a known height, so its apparent height in pixels gives the distance (pinhole camera model):

```
distance = stick height x focal length (px) / stick height in the image (px)
```

- **Stick height**: 7 ft by default; 8 ft or a custom value in settings.
- **Focal length**: browsers do not expose it. It is expressed as a ratio of the long side of the video frame (default 0.75, a typical 24-26 mm-equivalent main camera). A one-minute **calibration** (measure a door or stick of known height from a known distance) replaces the guess.
- **Camera tilt**: the stick is foreshortened by cos(elevation) when looking up or down; the phone's orientation sensor corrects for it. Phone **roll** is cancelled by rotating the analysed crop so a plumb stick is vertical.

### Finding the stick (no neural network, on purpose)

`src/features/monocle/vision/detector.ts`. The user aims at the flag, so only a narrow crop around the aiming box is analysed (a few milliseconds, in a Web Worker).

1. **Ridge map**: pixels brighter or darker than both horizontal neighbours, at three widths, each normalised by the local noise (per 64-row band, so a smooth sky does not make turf look like a line).
2. **Run search**: a 1-D max-sum search up each column (at a few small tilt angles) finds the strongest uninterrupted vertical run.
3. **Endpoints**: a maximum-likelihood step-edge fit to sub-pixel precision. The top is taken from the top of the flag when one is clearly there, because the flag dims the line response where it hangs.
4. **Flag evidence**: a flag-sized, differently coloured blob beside the top of the line raises confidence. It is a bonus, never a requirement (a flag seen edge-on is still measured).

Why not a trained model: there is no off-the-shelf flagstick detector, and even a good one returns a box, not a pixel-accurate top and base, which is what the distance error depends on. The detector sits behind `DetectorClient`, so a trained model can replace or assist it later. WebGPU/WebAssembly were not needed: the crop is small enough that plain JavaScript is fast (about 12 ms per frame on a laptop, a few times that on phones).

### Steadiness and confidence

`engine/stabilizer.ts`, `engine/confidence.ts`. Per-frame readings are pooled, outliers dropped, and the number is only shown ("locked") once the standard error of the estimate is small and most recent samples agree. The displayed integer only moves when the estimate has clearly moved, so 86, 87, 87, 88, 87 reads 87. Windows stretch automatically on slow devices (a phone analysing 3 frames a second locks more slowly instead of never). While not locked the app says **LOCKING ON…**.

Three separate things are reported and never conflated:

| | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | Is there a stick, and how sure | confidence (internal; gates the display) |
| Distance | The pinhole result | the big number |
| Accuracy | Likely error | the "±" next to it |

### Tap to bracket

When there is no lock for 4 seconds (or the flag top is hard to see), **TAP TO BRACKET** freezes the sharpest of the last few frames (least blur and rotation) and magnifies it. Drag the gold handle to the top of the flag and the green one to the base; a loupe shows the pixels under your finger, and each end snaps to a clear edge within 2.5 px on release. Same maths, no typing of distances. Hand movement does not matter once the frame is frozen.

## Accuracy (read this)

These numbers come from **synthetic frames** (sub-pixel stick width, optical blur, turf texture, sensor noise, at known distances) generated by `testing/synthScene.ts`. They show what the algorithm does under stated assumptions; they are **not field results**. Real optics, wind-blown flags, shadows and clutter will be worse in ways the synthetic scenes do not capture. Re-run with `npm run test:accuracy`.

Root-mean-square distance error from the pixel measurement alone (8 frames each, calibrated focal length and correct stick height, flag on the stick):

| Distance | Grass | Dark trees behind |
|---|---|---|
| 25 yd | 0.2% | 0.2% |
| 40 yd | 0.4% | 0.3% |
| 60 yd | 0.7% | 0.7% |
| 80 yd | 1.8% | 2.0% |
| 100 yd | 2.9% | 1.3% |
| 120 yd | 4.4% (found 5 of 8) | 4.6% (found 5 of 8) |

On top of that, systematic error that no image processing removes:

- **Wrong stick height**: 8 ft read as 7 ft is 14% short. Default assumption adds about 5% uncertainty; setting it removes most of that.
- **Uncalibrated focal length**: about 7% uncertainty. Calibration reduces it to about 2%.
- Combined, at 100 yd: roughly **±5 yd calibrated** and **±9 yd uncalibrated** (1 sigma). The "±" shown to the user is this estimate.

Beyond about 120 yd the stick is under 30 px tall at 1080p and is found only some of the time. That lines up with the product's 120 yd limit, but it means "over 120" is detected less reliably than "under 100".

### Fundamental limits of a single camera

- It measures the stick, not the ground. A raised green, a ridge hiding the base, or a stick leaning in the wind biases the number.
- A pale stick against **bright sky** can lose its top (no contrast). The app flags this ("flag top hard to see") and will not lock; use tap to bracket and put the handle on the flag.
- A dark spot at the base (cup, shadow) can add a pixel or two to the length; this has not been measured on real footage.
- Heat shimmer, rain on the lens and very low sun reduce contrast.
- Phone cameras apply electronic stabilisation that crops the frame, which shifts the real focal length. This is why calibration matters.
- 1 pixel of endpoint error at 100 yd is about 3 yd. More pixels on the stick (a telephoto lens, or higher resolution) is the only real fix; devices that benchmark fast request 1440p automatically.

## What the user sees

Full-screen rear camera, a tall narrow aiming box, a large white yardage on a solid dark fade (readable in sun), the wedge and swing underneath, and a single status line.

- `LOCKING ON…`, `HOLD STEADY`, `TOO DARK`, `TOO MUCH GLARE`, `STEP BACK`, `TARGET TOO FAR TO MEASURE`, `NO FLAG FOUND. CENTER IT IN THE BOX`, `FLAG TOP HARD TO SEE`
- Over 120 yd: **120+** and **DON'T SWITCH TO WEDGES YET**, no club. The detected target is still marked.
- Older phone notice (informational, never blocks): "Your camera may not be accurate enough for reliable distance measurements. For best results, use a newer smartphone." Shown for a low-resolution or slow camera, or when measured per-frame processing is slow.

## Wedge recommendations

`config/wedges.ts` (defaults, **placeholders**) and `engine/recommend.ts` (pure, driven entirely by config). Each wedge has full, 3/4 and 1/2 swing carry distances. The engine picks the shot with the closest carry, plus a small configurable penalty for partial swings so the fuller swing wins ties. Users can edit their own numbers in the settings sheet (stored in `monocle_settings.wedge_config`). The 120 yd cut-off is `maxYards` in the same config.

Extension points for later: wind, elevation, temperature and slope adjustments belong in `recommendWedge`'s input (adjust the distance before choosing); personalised or AI picks replace `recommendWedge` behind the same `Recommendation` type.

## Lucky Clovers

Server-driven, in `supabase/migrations/20261006180000_monocle.sql`:

- `monocle_request_clover()` decides if a clover appears, using the active row in `clover_campaigns` (spawn chance, poll interval, minimum gap, lifetime, daily cap, reward, bonus multiplier, rare chance and rare multiplier, start and end dates, priority). One live clover per user.
- `monocle_collect_clover(id)` collects once, atomically: owner only, not expired, not already collected, not tapped faster than a person can, under the daily cap (UTC day). It credits the player through the app's existing `add_clovers(p_user_id, p_amount)` function, so the clover lands in `golfer_profiles.clovers` like every other clover and follows whatever rules that function enforces. If `add_clovers` refuses, the whole collect rolls back and the clover stays collectable.
- Clients cannot insert or edit clovers or campaigns.

The app only asks, places the clover beside the aiming box (never over the aiming box, readout, buttons or edges; `clovers/spawnPosition.ts`), and sends the tap. After a collect it refreshes the profile so the balance updates everywhere. (Monocle is behind sign-in like the rest of the app, so signed-out teasers only appear if the route is ever made public.)

Run a promotion by editing a row, for example a double-clover weekend:

```sql
insert into public.clover_campaigns (slug, name, priority, starts_at, ends_at, multiplier, spawn_chance, rare_chance)
values ('double-weekend', 'Double Clover Weekend', 10, '2026-10-10', '2026-10-12', 2.0, 0.8, 0.15);
```

## Privacy

Camera frames are processed in memory on the device and discarded. Nothing is stored or uploaded. `monocle_sessions` holds one summary row per session (duration, device class, counters). The camera is released when the tab is hidden or the screen is left.

## Supported devices and browsers

**Not yet verified on a physical phone.** Everything so far was exercised in a desktop browser (mobile viewport) using the synthetic scene, plus the automated tests. Treat the list below as what the code is built for, and confirm it with the field-test protocol before launch.

- iPhone: Safari 15.4 or later (iOS 16+ recommended) for `requestVideoFrameCallback` and module workers, including installed (Add to Home Screen). Motion permission is requested from the Start tap.
- Android: Chrome 90 or later. Only the camera permission prompt.
- Needs HTTPS (or `localhost`), `getUserMedia`, Web Workers and canvas. Anything else shows an explanation instead of a broken screen. Without `requestVideoFrameCallback` it falls back to `requestAnimationFrame`; without workers it runs on the main thread.
- Rear camera required (a front camera is rejected outside debug mode).
- Desktop browsers work for development with a webcam or the synthetic scene, but are not a target.
- Not used: WebXR depth, LiDAR, course data.

iOS note: an app added to the home screen may ask for camera permission each launch; web apps cannot avoid that.

## Testing

```bash
npm test                 # all unit and integration tests (about 130)
npm run test:accuracy    # prints the accuracy tables above
npm run typecheck
npm run dev:https        # HTTPS dev server reachable from a phone on your network
```

Tests cover: geometry and sensor maths, distance and accuracy estimation, calibration, the recommendation engine, the stabilizer, confidence, the detector on synthetic scenes (known distances, tilt, off-centre, bare pole, sky and pole failure modes, tap-to-bracket snapping), the end-to-end pipeline to the text on screen, clover placement, the accuracy log, and the **Supabase migration against a real in-process Postgres (pglite)**: double-collect, other users, expiry, daily cap, rare rewards, row-level security.

### Debug and field-testing mode

Off for normal users. Dev builds: add `?monocle_debug=1`. Production builds: set `VITE_MONOCLE_DEBUG_TOKEN` and use `?monocle_debug=<token>` (with no token set, debug is off in production). It shows camera size and fps, analysis rate, per-frame processing time, device tier and benchmark, sensor values, the raw and smoothed distance, confidence components, detector candidates, and the recommendation output. A **known-distance test** logs shown versus laser distance and reports mean, RMS and max error, with CSV and JSON export.

Extra debug options: `&monocle_source=synthetic&dist=87` runs the built-in scene instead of the camera (a distance slider appears), and a button plays a recorded video file as the camera.

**Field test protocol**: calibrate; stand at 30, 50, 70, 90 and 110 yd from a flagstick measured with a laser; at each, hold steady for 3 seconds and log; repeat on grass, against trees and against sky. Please share the CSV; it is the data needed to retune thresholds on real optics.

## Production checklist

1. **Apply the migration** (`supabase/migrations/20261006180000_monocle.sql`) to the Supabase project. It is not applied automatically. It first checks that `golfer_profiles` (with `user_id`, `clovers`, `total_clovers`) and `add_clovers(p_user_id, p_amount)` exist and refuses to run, changing nothing, if they do not. This repo does not contain the live schema, so that check is the safety net. Until it is applied the rangefinder works and clovers are quietly skipped.
2. Optionally set `VITE_MONOCLE_DEBUG_TOKEN` in the Vercel project for production debugging.
3. Serve over HTTPS (Vercel does). The service worker precaches the app shell only and never caches Supabase calls; it is disabled in dev and inside iframes. `vercel.json`'s catch-all rewrite only applies when no file exists, so `sw.js`, the manifest and icons are served as files.
4. Review the placeholder wedge distances in `config/wedges.ts` and the default campaign row.
5. No API keys or model files are needed.

## Native packaging later

Camera access is behind `device/camera.ts` and the detector behind `vision/detectorClient.ts`, so a Capacitor or native plugin can supply real lens metadata (true focal length, the telephoto lens at about 3x the precision) and frames without touching the engine, UI or Supabase code.
